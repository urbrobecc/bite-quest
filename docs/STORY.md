# Bite Quest — canonical story

New York has a line problem. A handful of viral restaurants pull enormous crowds while thousands of extraordinary independent businesses go undiscovered a block away. A Uyghur bakery, a Trinidadian roti shop, a Georgian bread counter, an Oaxacan tamale cart: most New Yorkers walk past them every day and never go in.

Bite Quest turns those businesses into the game board. The city has cooking from well over a hundred countries, often clustered block by block, and most people only ever eat from ten of them. The game exists to change that number.

Players explore New York one cuisine at a time. Kazakh beshbarmak in Brighton Beach, Lao larb in Elmhurst, Ghanaian waakye in the Bronx, Salvadoran pupusas in Corona. Each first bite unlocks a badge for that country. There are hundreds to collect. Some are easy. Some almost nobody has: the Kyrgyz badge, the Nauruan badge, the badge for a country with a single kitchen in the five boroughs. The rarity is the point.

Badges stack into bigger trails. A full sweep of West African cuisines. Every Central Asian country. Ten dishes built on one ingredient. A whole neighborhood's worth of family-run spots. Finishing a trail says something real about how you eat and how much of the city you've actually seen.

Every listing centers the business, not just the plate: who owns it, where the family is from, how long they've held that corner, the dish they're known for, the story behind it. Finding the place counts as much as eating there.

The experience should feel playful, social, and rewarding, like exploring a city-sized game board with friends. You compare collections, send each other to places you've found, and race to reach a rare badge first. The payoff for playing is a real relationship with the city: more neighborhoods, more cultures, and more small businesses still open next year because people finally walked in.

## Content integrity in this prototype

The narrative above is the founder's product vision, not a verified census, demographic survey, or claim that every named cuisine currently has a NYC restaurant. In-app copy treats 100+ as an exploration goal, and ten as a player's possible rotation rather than a measured population average. The catalog supports ISO countries and territories; listing coverage starts with six real businesses. Do not imply a country badge guarantees a currently available NYC kitchen. Rarity counts supplied for demonstration are explicitly marked demo estimates and must be replaced by dated, sourced citywide inventories before presenting rarity as fact. An owner’s nationality must never be inferred from the cuisine. Simulated visits are not verified dining.


## Cuisine-first product direction

Lead with food, people, and independent neighborhood businesses. Collect Chinese Food, Laotian Food, Venezuelan Food, and other cuisine stamps. Country associations remain internal trail metadata, not a claim that cuisine maps neatly to nationality. Existing progress identifiers remain stable.

Restaurant profiles use short sourced prose and omit unknown facts. Never invent a family history. Share cards reuse the collection artwork, point friends toward a specific kitchen, and label simulated dining. No invented friends, engagement counts, or social feed.
